## Leave room for an interruption

A walk is different when it does not need to produce a result. A shop has changed its sign; a familiar corner sounds quieter; someone has planted flowers beside a bus stop. None of these observations needs to become a project.

## Keep one detail

Write down the detail that remained after returning home. One concrete sentence is often more useful than a complete account of the route. A notebook can hold an unfinished thought without asking for an explanation.

This is a standalone entry, outside either Journal series. It belongs in the same publication stream without needing a numbered place in a longer argument.

### Notice what changed

A familiar route can still hold a surprise. Stay with one small detail long enough to see it clearly, rather than collecting everything at once.

> A notebook can keep a moment without turning it into a task.

#### Leave a little space

An unfinished observation is allowed to remain unfinished. Return when there is something more to say.

- Keep the concrete detail.
- Leave room for a different interpretation.
