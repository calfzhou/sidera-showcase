A little marginalia can make a saved connection useful again. Keep a reason beside
the link, not a second copy of the destination.

## Give a link a reason

Two notes can mention the same tool while answering different questions. A useful connection says why the destination matters here: an example, a limitation, a competing explanation or a next step.

## Keep the surrounding sentence

A bare collection of links quickly becomes another reading queue. A sentence beside each important link preserves the original reason for saving it. That small amount of context makes the reference easier to revisit months later.

The earlier parts established a small writing habit and a habit of returning. Connecting selected notes builds on both without requiring every page to join a grand system. The final part will look at what to keep—and what to leave alone.

## Revisit a connected note

Return to [the reading list](../../notes/reading-list/index.md#follow-a-useful-connection)
or inspect [a small code specimen](../../notes/code-inclusion/index.md#read-just-the-loop).
Repeated mentions still describe one relationship, not several different sources.

### Connections across languages

The phrase 连接笔记 means connecting notes. A café notebook can keep both phrases
without turning a search into a translation service. Literal notation such as
`a+b[0]` is text, not a regular expression.
